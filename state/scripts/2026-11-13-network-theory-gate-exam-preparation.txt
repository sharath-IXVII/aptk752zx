"The first thing we need to clear up is a common misconception. Many of you might think network theory is just about memorizing formulas for resistors in series and parallel. But real network theory is about understanding the flow of current and the orientation of every single component. A mistake I see time and time again in student submissions is the incorrect representation of polarity. Let's get this right from the start. If you have an electrolytic capacitor in your circuit, the marking, usually a stripe or a minus sign, must connect to the NEGATIVE side. The + side of the electrolytic capacitor must connect to the higher voltage point. If you draw a connection that the circuit description does not support, or if you reverse that capacitor, the entire analysis falls apart. Similarly, for a diode, the arrow represents the direction of conventional current flow. The current enters the anode and exits the cathode. Never draw a connection the script does not describe, and never assume the orientation. Precision is everything in gate exam preparation.

"Now, let's talk about the specific question that plagues so many. Imagine a circuit with a direct current source, a resistor, and a capacitor. "But let's address the elephant in the room. The most common mistake I encounter is the incorrect handling of dependent sources. Students often forget that a dependent source, say a voltage-controlled voltage source, depends on another voltage or current in the circuit. If you are solving a problem and you see a source that says '10 times the voltage across the 2-ohm resistor', you cannot simply ignore that resistor or treat it as fixed. You must set up your mesh equations carefully, ensuring the controlling variable is correctly identified. A myth I want to bust right now: 'You can always simplify the circuit first.' Not true. If you simplify a circuit containing a dependent source before finding the controlling variable, you will lose the dependency and get the wrong answer. Always find the controlling variable first, then simplify.
